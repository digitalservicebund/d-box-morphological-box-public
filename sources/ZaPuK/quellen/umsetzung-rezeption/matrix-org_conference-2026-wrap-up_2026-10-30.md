## 🔗 Civil servants in Europe and beyond look to Matrix for their digital sovereignty needs

"Digital sovereignty is no longer a buzzword, but procurement policy," noted Jens-Uwe Thieme in a talk on Germany's Matrix-based TI-Messenger. And this is true: in June, the European Commission unveiled a European Tech Sovereignty Package , in response to both financial and strategic risks from proprietary software solutions. The EU identified some €264 billion is being spent every year on services. It had earlier recognised the strategic risk of governments not being able to control communications, in 2025 recommending EU governments consider Matrix for their real-time communication. Just before the start of The Matrix Conference, the Starlink satellite internet service throttled Ukrainian access to the internet to try and halt its strikes against Russia, underlining the profound risk governments take by trusting key communications components to private companies.

So, it was not surprising that governments and intergovernmental agencies were out in force at this year's Matrix Conference to talk about Matrix deployments at various stages of development. Representatives from the German Federal IT Cooperation (FITKO), the Swedish Social Insurance Agency (Försäkringskassan), the French Interministerial Directorate for Digital Affairs (DINUM), and the government of Luxembourg came to talk about why governments are increasingly choosing Matrix . The United Nations International Computing Centre (UNICC) offered insights from their journey to serve 100 UN entities with sovereign, federated communications, and the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) discussed the challenges of scaling up adoption of Matrix-based NI2CE messenger as an alternative to WhatsApp and Signal. We also had the opportunity to learn from the German experience deploying Matrix in healthcare and security, and to hear about new Matrix deployments in development in Brazil and the Netherlands .

Regulated industries – such as health insurers, financial institutions or legal enterprises – that have strict rules about handling personally identifiable information and a need to comply with privacy and data sovereignty laws, are also a natural match for Matrix-based solutions. At this year's conference we heard in particular from the health sector, including moving Germany's health insurer to TI-Messenger, the adoption of TI-Messenger in Austria , and the challenge of getting time-limited healthcare workers to adopt new messaging applications, even where required.
