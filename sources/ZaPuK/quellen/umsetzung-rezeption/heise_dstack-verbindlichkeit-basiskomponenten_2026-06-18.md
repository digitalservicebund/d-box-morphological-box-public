# Federal government and states celebrate breakthrough in binding commitment

The 16 federal states have pledged to introduce basic services of the Deutschland-Stack nationwide. However, this currently only involves a few components.

(Image: Ulrike Weis / KI / heise medien)

- Christian Wölbert

The federal government and the states are celebrating a breakthrough in negotiations over the Deutschland-Stack: The 16 federal states have pledged “binding connection and nationwide coverage” of basic components of the stack. In return, the federal government will largely bear the costs for the development and operation of the components. This represents a “decisive course has been set,” according to a press release from the IT Planning Council of the federal government and the states, published on Wednesday.

So far, however, it only concerns a manageable number of basic components. The states' commitment to connection, nationwide coverage, and usage applies to the already defined components, explained Markus Richter, State Secretary in the Federal Ministry for Digital Affairs, at the press conference following the IT Planning Council meeting and on LinkedIn . These include the digital identity (eID and EUDI Wallet ), the National Once Only Technical System (NOOTS) for retrieving proof data, and the data transport infrastructure FIT-Connect .

However, authorities are already obliged to accept eID and (from 2027) EUDI Wallet as part of the Online Access Act. And the binding nature of NOOTS is already regulated in the NOOTS Intergovernmental Agreement. Therefore, the new aspects essentially concern the regulations for the financing and binding nature of FIT-Connect. In many cases, however, interfaces for this transport infrastructure must be built by private software providers, who are not obliged to do so. The commitment of the federal government and the states to FIT-Connect could help to further establish this product and push back older infrastructures like OSCI.

### Payment service and mailbox system not yet ready

The Deutschland-Stack currently includes two further basic components: a payment service based on ePayBL and a comprehensive system for communication between the state, citizens, and companies based on Matrix, currently operating under the project name ZaPuK . According to Richter, these two components are still “under development.” What exactly the states have committed to here is still unclear, as the IT Planning Council's decision is only to be published in the coming days.

Especially with a nationwide rollout of ePayBL and ZaPuK, the Deutschland-Stack could have a noticeable impact. Currently, there is a federal patchwork of payment services. And due to the failure of De-Mail, there is currently no comprehensive system for legally compliant communication between citizens, companies, and the state. The federal government and the states intend to gradually add more components to the Deutschland-Stack. For this purpose, according to Richter, a “Portfolio Board” is to be established, in which the federal government and five states will oversee further development and federal use.

From the perspective of those involved, they have nevertheless already achieved a breakthrough. “The Gordian knot has been cut,” said Richter at the press conference. State Secretary and IT Planning Council member Fedor Rose from Rhineland-Palatinate commented that he was enthusiastic about the binding nature of the decision. “The states will use the basic components bindingly and roll them out nationwide!” In December, Bavaria was the only state to vote against a decision on the binding use of the stack. At that time, the components were not yet definitively defined.

### Expert calls for quality criteria

From the perspective of Florian Theißing, an expert on digital government at the think tank Agora Digitale Transformation, the decision is “a huge step towards a unified platform for digital administration, which we urgently need.” However, the obligation to use must also be accompanied by binding quality criteria for the components. “It would be fatal if the authorities were forced by the decision to use unsuitable solutions,” Theißing emphasized in an interview with c't. “The BundID is a cautionary example here.” Just recently, North Rhine-Westphalia and Bavaria criticized the usability of the BundID and the associated eID at the Conference of Digital Ministers.

In addition to the basic components, the Deutschland-Stack also includes a catalog of technical standards , which the federal government and the states agreed upon in the spring. This is also to be further developed.
